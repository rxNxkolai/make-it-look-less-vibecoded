# Typography

Type carries the personality of the page. It is the cheapest place to stop looking AI-generated and the first place reviewers check.

## What to avoid, and why

- Inter, Roboto, Arial, Open Sans, Lato, Poppins, Geist, or Space Grotesk as the entire identity. Not bad fonts; they are the statistical default, so alone they read as "nobody chose this". Space Grotesk deserves a special flag: models converge on it even when told to be creative.
- Weight 700 headlines with tight tracking as the only hierarchy move. The weight is the giveaway more than the font. Try 500-600 for display, or go extreme (200 against 800).
- Font-size-only hierarchy. Use weight, width, case, and color together.
- The lone serif-italic accent word inside an all-sans page (and the Space Grotesk + Instrument Serif pairing that carries it). Now a cliche.
- Bold-only emphasis. Italics exist; real typesetting uses them for titles, terms, and voice.
- Typefaces with fewer than five weights; you will run out of hierarchy.
- Playfair Display for anything editorial: it has become the "tasteful default" and reads as templated in that context.

## Rules

1. Two faces, chosen as a pair: a characterful display face used with restraint, plus a complementary body face. Add a utility face (mono or condensed) only when data or captions call for it.
2. Hierarchy from contrast: big weight jumps (200 vs 800), size jumps of 3x or more between display and body, one mathematical scale (x1.25 for apps, x1.333 for editorial).
3. Body at 16px minimum; measure 60-80 characters; line-height around 1.5 for body, tighter (1.1-1.2) for display.
4. Prefer variable fonts; load only the axes you use.
5. Letter-spacing: slightly negative for large display, slightly positive for small caps and labels, never default-everywhere.
6. Set real typographic details when the direction warrants: proper quotes, hanging punctuation, tabular numbers for data.

## Pairings that read as chosen (all free on Google Fonts)

| Pairing | Reads as |
|---|---|
| Fraunces + Source Serif 4 | warm editorial |
| Bricolage Grotesque + Fraunces | characterful modern |
| DM Serif Display + DM Sans | classic product |
| Libre Caslon Text + Work Sans | literary, calm |
| Chivo Mono + Fraunces (roles reversed) | technical-editorial |
| Archivo Expanded + Archivo | Swiss, loud display |
| IBM Plex Mono + IBM Plex Sans | industrial, systems |
| Instrument Serif + a NON-grotesk body | fine, but break the cliche pairing |

Rotate these too. The point is never a specific font; it is that someone visibly made a choice that fits the brief. When the direction is brutalist, system fonts used on purpose (Arial at 900, Times at display size) beat any curated pairing.

## Per-direction quick picks

- Editorial print: high-contrast serif display, readable serif or humanist sans body.
- Swiss minimal: one grotesk family, extreme size and weight range, nothing else.
- Industrial mono: one mono family for everything, hierarchy from case and color.
- Warm organic: soft serif or rounded sans display, humanist body.
- Dense utility: compact sans or mono, tabular numbers, 13-14px acceptable for data tables only.
