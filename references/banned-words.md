# Banned words and phrases (v0.1.0, stamped 2026-07-24)

How to use this file: these are defaults to avoid, not illegal words. Each entry is statistically overrepresented in LLM output; none is wrong in itself. Replace with a plain word, or keep it only when it is genuinely the precise term (a "robustness check" in a stats writeup is fine; a "robust platform" in a hero is not). This list drifts by model generation; re-stamp the date when editing.

Sources merged: Wikipedia "Signs of AI writing" (WikiProject AI Cleanup), the berenslab/llm-excess-vocab academic list, arXiv:2412.11385's 21 focal words, and published writer/marketer lists. Roughly 250 entries.

## Structural bans (worth more than any single word)

- "-ing" tailing clauses that fake analysis: "...highlighting its importance", "...underscoring its significance", "...paving the way for".
- "not just X, but Y" and other negative parallelisms.
- Rule-of-three adjective triplets ("fast, simple, and powerful").
- Em-dash chains; more than one em dash per screen of copy.
- Vague attribution ("experts say", "studies show") without a named source.
- A compulsive closing summary restating what the section just said.
- Exclamation inflation; Title Case On Every Heading; emoji bullets.
- Invented numbers of any kind: metrics, user counts, star ratings, testimonials.

## Replacement direction

Plain verbs win: use, make, build, start, show, cut, save, send, get. Say what the thing does in the user's terms, name the concrete object, and stop. Specific beats clever, always.
