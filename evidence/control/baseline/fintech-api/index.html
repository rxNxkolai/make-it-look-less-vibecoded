01 / The problem

Nobody sets out to build a bank. It happens anyway.

You started with one payout flow. Three years later you have a NACHA file generator, a Slack channel for stuck wires, and a finance team that closes the month by hand. Here is how it usually goes.

  1. 01

    Every rail is a separate integration

    6 to 14 months per rail

    ACH is a fixed width file. RTP is ISO 20022. Your UK bank wants SFTP and a PGP key that rotates quarterly. Each one arrives with its own cutoff times, return codes, and a partner bank contact who answers email on Tuesdays. The code is not the hard part. The operational surface is.

  2. 02

    Balances live in three places and agree in none

    Reconciliation drift

    Your app database says the merchant has $412,900. The bank statement says something else. The processor's API says a third number, and it is eventually consistent. Without a real ledger you are diffing systems that were never designed to agree, and every discrepancy becomes an archaeology project.

  3. 03

    The audit surprises you every single time

    SOC 2 and state MTL

    Money movement pulls in obligations you did not scope: immutable audit trails, segregated settlement accounts, sanctions screening, and evidence that a given cent was where you claim it was on a given date. Retrofitting that into a system built for product velocity is the expensive way to learn it.

02 / Capabilities

Six primitives. Everything else composes from them.

Trestle exposes a small object model on purpose. Counterparties, accounts, ledger entries, payments, events, and reports. If you know one endpoint you can guess the rest.

Payouts

Push funds to any counterparty on the fastest rail that satisfies your constraints, or pin the rail yourself. Automatic retry across rails on soft failure.

  • Smart rail selection by cost, speed, limit
  • Batch endpoint, 25,000 per request
  • Scheduled and recurring disbursements

Collections

Pull funds with ACH debit, SEPA Direct Debit, BACS DD, and open banking payment initiation. Mandate storage and return handling included.

  • Mandate lifecycle and proof of authorization
  • R-code and return reason normalization
  • Balance checks before debit where supported

Ledger

A real double-entry ledger with immutable entries, atomic multi-leg transactions, and balances you can query at any historical timestamp.

  • Serializable transactions, no partial writes
  • Balance as of any point in time
  • Custom account hierarchies per tenant

Reconciliation

Bank statements ingested continuously and matched against ledger entries. Exceptions surface as typed objects, not as a mystery in a spreadsheet.

  • Continuous BAI2, MT940, and camt.053 ingest
  • Deterministic matching with audit reasons
  • Break queue with assignment and notes

FX and multi-currency

Hold balances in 24 currencies. Lock a rate for up to 60 seconds, or settle at the interbank mid rate plus a disclosed spread.

  • Quote objects with explicit expiry
  • Spread shown per quote, never bundled
  • Same day conversion on 11 corridors

Events and webhooks

Every state change emits a signed, ordered event. Replay any window up to 30 days, and inspect delivery attempts in the dashboard.

  • HMAC signed, timestamped, replay safe
  • Per endpoint ordering guarantees
  • One click replay by time range or type
03 / Integration

Three calls from empty repo to settled funds.

Sandbox keys are issued instantly and simulate real rail behaviour, including returns, cutoffs, and weekend settlement gaps.

1

Create a counterparty

Bank details are tokenized on receipt. Trestle validates routing numbers, IBANs, and sort codes against live directories before the object is returned.

const cp = await trestle
  .counterparties.create({
    name: "Halcyon Ltd",
    country: "GB",
    account: {
      iban: "GB29NWBK6016…"
    }
  });
2

Fund the settlement account

Pull from your operating account or receive inbound on a virtual account. The ledger records both legs the moment funds are confirmed.

await trestle.transfers.create({
  from: "acct_operating",
  to:   "acct_settlement",
  amount: 2_500_000,
  currency: "gbp",
  rail: "fps"
});
3

Pay out and listen

Submit the payout with an idempotency key, then react to payout.settled or payout.returned on your webhook endpoint.

app.post("/hooks", (req, res) => {
  const e = trestle.webhooks
    .verify(req, SECRET);

  if (e.type === "payout.settled")
    markPaid(e.data.id);

  res.sendStatus(200);
});

05 / Ledger

Double entry, or it did not happen.

Most payment APIs give you a status field. Trestle gives you the accounting. Every movement writes balanced entries in a single serializable transaction, and nothing is ever updated in place.

The number is the same everywhere, because there is only one number.

Balances are derived from entries, not stored and patched. When you ask for a merchant balance as of 14:22:07 last Tuesday, you get the balance as of 14:22:07 last Tuesday, computed from immutable history.

That property is what makes disputes short. You are not reconstructing what probably happened. You are reading what did.

  • ATOMICMulti leg transactions commit fully or not at all, across any number of accounts and currencies.
  • IMMUTABLEEntries are append only. Corrections are reversing entries with a link to the original.
  • QUERYABLEPoint in time balances, trial balance exports, and per tenant account trees.
  • EXPORTABLEJournal exports to NetSuite, Xero, and QuickBooks on a schedule you set.

Trestle is not a bank. We hold direct rail memberships where they are available to non-banks, and we work with four partner banks across the US, UK, and EU for the rest. Customer funds sit in FBO accounts at those banks. Which bank backs a given account is visible in the API on the settlement_bank field, because you should never have to ask us that in a support ticket.
If you specified a rail explicitly, the payout stays queued and you get a payout.delayed event with the expected recovery window. If you used smart selection, we route to the next rail that satisfies your constraints on speed, cost, and limit, and the substitution is recorded on the payout object. Rail health is published in real time on the status page and available as an API resource.
Sandbox to first successful payout is usually under a day. A production launch covering one region, webhook handling, and reconciliation typically runs two to four weeks including your own review cycles. The long pole is almost never our API, it is your onboarding and KYB flow for counterparties, which is why we ship a hosted version of it.
