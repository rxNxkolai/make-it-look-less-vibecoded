What a payment does between authorize and settle

Six states run in order. Everything else is a branch off one of them, and every branch writes its own reversing rows rather than editing the originals.

01 created The instruction exists and holds an idempotency key.
02 authorized The rail accepted it. Ledger rows are written here.
03 captured You took the amount, in full or in part.
04 in_transit Funds left the origin account and are on the rail.
05 settled The receiving bank confirmed value.
06 reconciled A statement line matched the ledger entry.

Send a payment

One call. Amounts are integers in the minor unit, so nothing rounds on the way in. The ledger rows come back on the response rather than on a webhook you have to wait for, and credits are negative, so the array sums to zero.

When it breaks

Money systems fail in a small number of ways, and all of them eventually happen. Here is what Weir does in each case and what is left for you.

Failure modes, the API response, and the work left to the caller
what happenswhat Weir doeswhat you do
Your request times out before a response arrives The idempotency key holds the outcome for 24 hours, whether it succeeded or not. Retry with the same key. You get the original payment back, never a second one.
Two workers send the same invoice at once The second call returns 409 with the id of the payment the first one made. Read conflict.payment and carry on with that id.
The rail returns the funds after authorization State moves to returned and reversing rows are appended. Nothing is edited or deleted. Subscribe to payment.returned. The balance corrects itself in the next query.
A card payment is disputed The amount moves to holding.disputes. The original rows stay where they are. Post evidence to /v1/disputes/:id/evidence before the deadline on the object.
You capture less than you authorized The remainder is released and the fee recalculates against the captured amount. Nothing. Both the capture and the release are already rows.
Your webhook endpoint is down Delivery retries for 72 hours on a widening gap, then the event waits in the log. Replay from /v1/events once you are back up. Ordering holds.

An error body names the two values that disagree and says which of the two things to change. Nothing on this page apologises to you, and nothing tells you that something went wrong without also telling you what to send next.

Every error carries a stable type string. Match on that, never on the message, because the message is written for the human reading your logs at 3am and will change when we can word it better.
