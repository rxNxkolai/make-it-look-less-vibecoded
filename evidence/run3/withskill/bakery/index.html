The night before your loaf

Reading it left to right: the bench is busy before dawn and empty by nine, the oven runs in four bursts, and the counter is open across all of it. The two short oven bars are focaccia at 10:40 and baguettes at 14:15. After four we wash down, feed the starter and shape tomorrow.

Flour, starter, and who is actually here

The white flour comes from Hazel Mill, forty minutes up the road, stoneground and sold to us in paper sacks rather than tankers. The rye is from the same place. We do not blend anything in to make the dough behave, so the loaves change a little through the year, which is the honest version of what flour does.

The starter is called Doris. She lives in a bucket beside the mixer and gets fed twice a day, once at three in the morning and once before we lock up.

Two of us bake and one of us serves. On Saturday there are three of us jammed behind the bench and it is louder than it needs to be.

If you want to know what is in something, ask at the counter. We can tell you every item in it, because there are usually four.
