Our story

A bakehouse, not a bakery chain.

In 2008 Maren Halloway signed a lease on a shuttered laundromat because it had a floor strong enough to hold a two-tonne oven. She had one mixer, a borrowed van, and a starter she had kept alive since culinary school.

The starter is still alive. It is called Ada, it lives in a crock by the north window, and it gets fed twice a day whether or not we are open. Every loaf we sell comes from it.

We have never used a dough conditioner, a bread improver, or a par-baked frozen anything. The trade-off is honest: we make a fixed number of things, and when they are gone, they are gone. That is not a marketing decision. It is how much a single oven can hold.

Maren Halloway Founder & head baker

How a loaf happens

Four days from flour to counter.

01

Feed Ada

Two feeds a day, twelve hours apart, at exactly the same hydration. Ada dictates the timetable. Everyone else works around her.

Day one · evening
02

Mix and fold

Flour and water rest for an hour before the salt goes in. Then four sets of folds over four hours. No machine touches it after the mixer.

Day two · morning
03

Shape and chill

Shaped by hand into bannetons, then into the cold room at 4°C for 48 hours. This is where the flavour actually comes from.

Day two · afternoon
04

Score and bake

Scored with a razor, loaded onto stone at 250°C with a burst of steam, baked 45 minutes until the crust is nearly too dark.

Day four · 3am
