01 The case

Most habit apps are trying to be your parent.

They gamify, they nag, they turn a missed Tuesday into a red screen and a lost 47 day streak. Then you delete the app, because losing feels worse than never having played.

Notch takes the opposite position. A habit tracker is a record, not a referee. Its job is to tell you the truth about the last six months in under two seconds, so you can make one honest decision about the next six.

That means fast entry, an honest visual, and a streak system that understands you will get sick, travel, and occasionally have a genuinely terrible week. None of that means you quit.

You do not need motivation. You need to be able to see the shape of what you have already done.

02 What it does

F.01

One tap, then close the app

Everything lives on a single screen. Open it, tap what you did, done. Median session time on Notch is under six seconds, which is exactly the point.

  • • Yes or no habits, counts, or durations
  • • Home screen widget with today's row
  • • Backfill yesterday without a guilt prompt
  • • Keyboard shortcuts on desktop, 1 through 9

Today, tap to log

F.02

Streaks that survive real life

Every habit gets rest days you define, plus two skip tokens a month. Miss a day inside your allowance and the streak holds. Miss it outside, and Notch records the gap plainly instead of wiping the slate.

  • • Set rest days per habit, not per app
  • • Two skip tokens monthly, they do not roll over
  • • Broken streaks stay visible in your history
  • • Zero push notifications about lost streaks

F.03

It shows you the pattern, not just the score

A percentage tells you nothing actionable. Notch breaks completion down by weekday, time of day, and season, so you can see that the habit is not failing, Wednesdays are.

  • • Weekday and hour breakdowns per habit
  • • Correlations between any two habits you track
  • • Month over month drift, plain numbers
  • • Plain CSV and JSON export, no paywall

Reading habit, completion by weekday

Ninety one percent on Mondays, thirty four percent on Wednesdays. The habit is fine. Wednesday evenings are the problem.

F.04

Reminders that read the room

One nudge, at a time that has historically worked for you, and nothing else. If you already logged the habit, the reminder never fires. If you have ignored the same nudge four days running, Notch asks whether to move it or drop it.

  • • Time based, location based, or after another habit
  • • Silent hours you set once and forget
  • • Auto suppressed when the habit is already done
  • • Hard cap of three notifications a day, total

04 Getting going

1

Name three habits

Three, not eleven. Notch caps the free tier at five on purpose, because tracking everything is how people stop tracking anything.

2

Set your rest days

Decide up front which days do not count against you. Saturdays off is a schedule, not a failure, and the streak math should know that.

3

Check in once a week

Daily tapping takes seconds. The useful part is the Sunday review, where the year view tells you what actually happened.

05 Rest days

A bad week is data, not a verdict.

Traditional streak counters reset to zero the moment you slip, which quietly teaches you that eleven good days were worth nothing. Notch counts the gap, then keeps going. Flip the switch and watch what the same month looks like under each system.

08 Questions

Fair things to ask.

Does Notch work offline?

Yes, and it is built that way first. Your data lives on the device in a local database, and sync is a layer on top rather than a requirement. Turn off the network for a month and nothing breaks, including the year view and every breakdown.

What happens to my data if I stop paying?

You drop to the Pocket tier and keep every record you ever made. Nothing is deleted, nothing is held hostage. Habits above the five limit go read only until you either pick five or upgrade again, and export stays available on every tier.

Can I import from another habit app?

There are direct importers for Streaks, Habitica, Loop, and Way of Life, plus a generic CSV importer that will take any file with a date column and a habit column. Bring years of history in and the year view fills in immediately.

Why cap the free tier at five habits?

Because tracking twenty habits is the most reliable way to abandon all twenty. Five is the number at which people in our own usage data are still logging six months later. If you genuinely need more, Ledger removes the cap, but try five first.

Do you sell or analyse my data?

No. Notch has no advertising, no third party analytics, and no data sharing agreements. Sync traffic is end to end encrypted, and we cannot read your habit names or entries. The business model is that you pay us money, which keeps things simple.

Which platforms are supported?

iOS and Android as native apps, macOS and Windows as desktop apps, and any modern browser on the web. Widgets on iOS and Android, a menu bar app on macOS, and a keyboard driven web version for people who never touch the mouse.

Is there an API?

There is a documented REST API and webhooks on the Ledger and Household plans, so you can log entries from a shortcut, a smart watch, a terminal, or your own automation. Read access to your full history is included on every paid tier.
