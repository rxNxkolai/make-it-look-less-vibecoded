No. 64 / July 2026

Cities

The Grain of a Neighbourhood

A street is not a plan. It is a texture you learn with your feet: which corner floods, which baker opens at six, which stairwell carries sound. I spent a year mapping one hillside in Lisbon the slow way, and found that almost nothing worth knowing about it appears on a map.

The city planners drew lines. The residents drew shortcuts. Forty years later the shortcuts are the streets, and the lines are where the weeds grow.

About

I started this site because a magazine cut my favourite paragraph.

That was 2014. The paragraph was about a woman who repaired umbrellas in a doorway near the station, and the editor was almost certainly right to cut it. But I wanted somewhere the digressions could survive, so I built one, badly, in an evening.

Since then I have written sixty-four essays here, three of which I would still defend in public. I work slowly. I report by walking, asking, and going back a second time to check what I got wrong the first time. My corrections page is longer than most people's about page, which I have decided to be proud of.

The book, if it ever finishes, is called Night Work. It is about the people who keep the sleeper trains running while everyone aboard is unconscious.
